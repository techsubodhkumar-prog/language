1. Introduction
1.1 Purpose
This document defines the functional and non-functional requirements for a mobile platform that helps people find others
for activities they want to do. Users can state their interests, discover relevant activities, create a plan, specify time and
place, invite or request participants, communicate with accepted participants, and manage the activity through completion.
1.2 Product Vision
Make it easy for a person to turn “I want to do this” into “I found the right people and we made a plan.” The platform
supports solo-to-group discovery, filling open spots in an existing group, creating new activities, and joining public or
private activities.
1.3 Intended Audience
l Product owner and stakeholders defining scope and priorities.
l UI/UX designers creating user flows, wireframes, and prototypes.
l Flutter developers building the mobile application.
l Backend developers implementing APIs, matching, chat, notifications, and data storage.
l QA engineers verifying requirements and acceptance criteria.
l Moderation, customer support, and operations teams managing safety and reports.

1.4 Definitions
Term Definition
Activity A planned event or action, such as cricket, tea, shopping, a trip, or a study session.
Host A user who creates and manages an activity.
Participant A user who requests to join or is accepted into an activity.
Domain/category A user interest area, such as sports, food, travel, games, hobbies, or dating/social.
Matching Filtering and ranking activities or users by interests, time, distance, eligibility, and preferences.

Poll A question with selectable options used to coordinate group decisions.

MVP Minimum Viable Product: the smallest safe and useful initial release.
2. Overall Description
2.1 Product Perspective
The product is a location-aware social coordination platform, not merely a feed or chat application. Its core workflow is
activity-first: users discover or create an activity, matching people are notified or can find it in discovery, interested users
request to join, the host approves or the system admits them according to the activity’s join policy, and participants
coordinate safely.
2.2 User Classes
User class Description Main permissions
Guest Not authenticated View introductory screens and sign in/register; no posting or messaging.
Registered user Person seeking or organizing activities Manage profile/interests, discover activities, request/join, create
activities, chat where permitted, report/block.
Host Registered user who created an activity Edit/cancel activity, review requests, manage participants, communicate
with participants, mark completion.
Moderator/support Authorized operational staff Review reports, investigate abuse, remove content, restrict accounts,
respond to support requests.
Administrator Trusted platform operator Manage categories, policies, roles, configuration, audit logs, and
platform metrics.
2.3 Supported Activity Categories
l Sports and fitness: cricket, football, badminton, gym, running, cycling.
l Food and casual outings: tea/coffee, restaurants, street food, brunch.
l Travel and outdoors: trips, trekking, camping, sightseeing, road trips.
l Entertainment: movies, concerts, events, bowling, amusement parks.
l Shopping and errands: mall visits, fashion shopping, markets, shared errands.
l Games: board games, multiplayer video games, LAN sessions, esports.
l Learning and careers: study groups, projects, hackathons, workshops, networking.
l Creative hobbies: photography, dance, music, filmmaking, art, book clubs.
l Social and relationships: making friends, group dates, double dates, singles' events.
l Family, community, volunteering, pets, and other user-suggested categories.
Categories must be configurable from the backend. Users may suggest a new category, but platform administrators or a
controlled taxonomy process should approve it to prevent duplicates and unsafe categories.

4. User Journey and Core Workflows
4.1 First-Time User
1 User opens the app and sees introductory/onboarding screens.
2 User registers or signs in and accepts the terms, privacy notice, and community/safety rules.
3 User creates a profile with display name, profile image (optional), age eligibility data as required, and a short bio.
4 User selects interests and sub-interests, for example Sports fi Cricket; Food fi Tea/Coffee; Travel fi Weekend trips.
5 User sets discovery preferences such as preferred activity radius, notification preferences, and optional availability.
6 App opens the personalized Home/Discover screen; the user can change interests and privacy settings at any time.
4.2 Example: Organize a Cricket Match
1 Host selects Create Activity fi Sports fi Cricket.
2 Host enters date, start/end time, venue or meeting point, player count, skill level (optional), cost per person (optional),
and rules.
3 Host chooses visibility (public or invite-only), join policy (approval required or auto-join), and target
audience/preferences.
4 System validates fields and publishes the activity.
5 Eligible users who follow cricket and meet the discovery criteria can see the activity; matching notifications are sent only
according to user preferences and rate limits.
6 Interested users open details and tap Request to Join or Join.
7 Host reviews requests when approval is required. System updates available spots atomically to prevent overbooking.
8 Accepted participants receive confirmation and can use the activity group chat.
9 System sends configurable reminders and notifies participants of schedule or venue changes.
10 Host marks the activity completed or cancelled; participants may optionally provide feedback or report a problem.
4.3 Example: Invite People for Tea/Coffee
1 User selects Create Activity fi Food & Drink fi Tea/Coffee.
2 User chooses cafe or public meeting point, date/time, participant limit, estimated spend, and whether the plan is
one-to-one or a small group.
3 The activity is shown to eligible users who opted into relevant social/food activities and match the visibility and
preference rules.
4 Interested users request to join; the host can review profile basics and accept or decline.
5 Contact details remain private unless a user deliberately shares them; communication occurs through the app after the
relevant join/approval condition.
6 Both sides can cancel, block, or report. Safety reminders encourage meeting in public and telling a trusted person
where appropriate.

6. Business Rules
l Every activity must have one host, a category, a start time, a location/meeting method, and a maximum participant
count.
l Minimum participants must be less than or equal to maximum participants. The minimum is an activity goal; the
maximum is a hard capacity limit.
l The host may optionally require a minimum number of participants before the activity is considered confirmed. The app
must show the confirmation status clearly.
l An activity can be public/discoverable, private/invite-only, or unlisted/share-link based. Visibility and invitation
permissions must be explicit.
l The platform must distinguish a user's interest from consent to receive direct messages or repeated invitations.
l Interest membership must not automatically expose phone number, email, exact location, or other private profile data.
l Users must be able to report or block without needing to confront the other user.
l For dating or romantic categories, participation must follow the product's age policy, consent rules, anti-harassment
policy, and applicable law. Dating must not be silently enabled for ineligible users.
l The platform must define cancellation behavior, no-show handling, waitlist behavior, and what happens when a host
account is suspended.
l Repeated invitations must be rate-limited; users must not be able to broadcast unlimited requests to everyone in a
domain.
l The system must prevent unauthorized users from viewing private activities, private chats, or pending requests.
l Activity recommendations must explain key relevance signals where practical and must not infer sensitive traits from
unrelated activity.

7.1 Important Data Constraints
l Unique membership constraint for (activity_id, user_id) to prevent duplicate membership.
l Capacity updates must use a transaction, lock, or equivalent atomic conditional update.
l All times must be stored consistently (prefer UTC) and rendered in the user's local timezone.
l Location should be stored at the minimum precision necessary; exact home addresses should not be required for
ordinary activities.
l Private message and report data must have strict authorization checks and retention rules.
l Deletion/anonymization must account for legal obligations, abuse prevention, and records that cannot lawfully be erased
immediately.

l Versioned HTTPS REST API (or equivalent documented API) for account, profile, interest, activity, membership, poll,
message, notification, and moderation resources.
l Push notification provider and optional email/SMS provider.
l Maps/geocoding provider for venue selection; avoid sending unnecessary personal data to third parties.
l Object storage/CDN for profile and activity images, with file-type/size validation and abuse scanning.

The app must show actionable, human-readable errors for network failure, expired session, permission denial, full activity,
closed activity, rejected request, invalid time, unavailable venue service, upload failure, and rate limiting. Retry actions
must not duplicate activities, memberships, messages, or payments.

10. Security, Privacy, and Trust & Safety
10.1 Security Controls
l Use a reputable authentication solution or correctly implemented server-side authentication; never store plaintext
passwords.
l Apply least-privilege role-based access for users, hosts, moderators, and administrators.
l Validate and authorize every activity, membership, chat, report, and admin request on the server.
l Protect against injection, broken access control, credential stuffing, spam, malicious uploads, and replay/duplicate
requests.
l Use rate limits, abuse monitoring, secure session revocation, and audit logs for privileged actions.
l Do not expose secrets in the Flutter app, source control, crash reports, or client-side environment files.

11. Recommended Technical Architecture
The architecture should keep the Flutter client thin and place matching, authorization, capacity checks, and safety
enforcement on the backend.

13. Acceptance Criteria for MVP
1 A new user can register, accept required policies, create a profile, select multiple interests, and reach the Discover
screen.
2 A user can create and publish a cricket activity with time, venue, capacity, and join policy.
3 A user who follows cricket can discover the activity when its visibility, filters, and eligibility rules permit.
4 An interested user can request to join; the host can approve or reject; both receive the correct status and notification.
5 The system never admits more accepted participants than the activity's configured capacity, including during concurrent
requests.
6 Approved participants can access the activity chat; non-members and unauthorized users cannot.
7 A host can edit or cancel an activity and accepted participants receive a clear update.
8 A user can create a tea/coffee outing and select a public cafe or meeting point without exposing their private home
address.
9 Users can block/report profiles and activities; the action is recorded and available to authorized moderators.
10 Users can edit interests and notification preferences; non-essential broadcast notifications respect these settings.
11 Critical workflows have automated tests and pass manual testing on the defined supported Android devices.
12 The production deployment has monitoring, backups, rate limiting, secure configuration, a privacy notice, community
guidelines, and an operational moderation process.

17. Product Success Metrics
l Activation: percentage of new users who complete onboarding and select at least one interest.
l Time to first value: time from registration to first activity view, join request, or created activity.
l Match success: percentage of activities that reach their minimum target before start time.
l Join conversion: activity detail views that become join requests or confirmed joins.
l Attendance/fulfillment: percentage of confirmed activities marked completed or attended.
l Retention: returning users at 7-day and 30-day intervals.
l Safety: report rate, time to first moderation action, repeat-abuse rate, and user block rate.
l Notification quality: opt-out rate, mute rate, and conversion from relevant notifications.
l Local liquidity: number of active activities and eligible participants by category and area.
Metrics should be aggregated with privacy safeguards. Do not optimize only for message volume or time spent; successful
real-world activity completion and user safety are more meaningful outcomes.
